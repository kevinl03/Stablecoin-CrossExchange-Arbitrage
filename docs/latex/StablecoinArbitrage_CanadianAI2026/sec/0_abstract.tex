\begin{abstract}
Cross-exchange cryptocurrency arbitrage enables low-risk profit from price discrepancies across exchanges, yet existing approaches predominantly employ negative cycle detection algorithms (e.g., Bellman--Ford) that target opportunity identification rather than execution feasibility. We introduce an execution-aware pathfinding system for stablecoin cross-exchange arbitrage using A* search with domain-specific heuristic functions. The problem is modelled as a weighted directed graph where nodes represent (exchange, stablecoin) pairs across 12 major centralized exchanges and edges encode trades and transfers with real-world execution costs—trading fees, order-book slippage, withdrawal fees, gas costs, transfer delays, and exchange reliability risks. Stablecoins, assets pegged to fiat currencies such as the US dollar, provide the trading medium due to their low volatility and high cross-venue liquidity. We propose three domain-specific guidance heuristics—$h_1$ (liquidity), $h_2$ (slippage), $h_4$ (chain congestion and exchange risk)—and a complementary multi-start search-control strategy $S_1$ that diversifies exploration across starting exchanges. A Weighted A* formulation fuses real-time market data with risk estimates, enabling the system to prioritize paths that balance profitability with execution safety. Extensive evaluation through deterministic experiments, a brute-force 3-hop enumeration baseline, Monte Carlo backtesting over 500 independent market snapshots, sensitivity analysis of heuristic hyperparameters, and multi-hour temporal data collection confirms that heuristic-guided A* finds optimal or near-optimal paths while substantially reducing node expansions relative to exhaustive enumeration, with the advantage increasing as the graph scales.
\end{abstract}
