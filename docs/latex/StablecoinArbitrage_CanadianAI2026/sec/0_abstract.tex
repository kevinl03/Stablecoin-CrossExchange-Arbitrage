\begin{abstract}
Cross-exchange cryptocurrency arbitrage enables low-risk profit from price discrepancies across exchanges. However, existing approaches predominantly employ negative cycle detection algorithms (e.g., Bellman-Ford) that focus on opportunity identification rather than execution feasibility. To address this gap, we introduce an execution-aware pathfinding system for stablecoin cross-exchange arbitrage using A* search with domain-specific heuristic functions. We model the problem as a weighted graph where nodes represent (exchange, stablecoin) pairs and edges encode trades and transfers with real-world execution costs, including trading fees, slippage, withdrawal fees, gas costs, transfer delays, and operational risks. A novel subset of cryptocurrency known as stable-coins, currency backed assets that are pegged to fiscal entities such as the US dollar, are the medium for our trading framework. A key challenge is that execution constraints must be evaluated during pathfinding to assess profitability and feasibility in real-time. To handle this, we propose four novel heuristic functions that guide A* search toward profitable and executable paths: H1 (liquidity-based), H2 (slippage-aware), H3 (parallel exploration), and H4 (chain congestion and exchange risk). In order to disambiguate across multiple execution paths, we introduce a weighted A* formulation that fuses real-time market data with risk estimates, enabling the system to prioritize paths that balance profitability with execution safety.
\end{abstract}
