\subsection{Results}

\subsubsection{Deterministic Heuristic Comparison}

On the cached graph (41~nodes, 864~edges, 30~start nodes, three cash levels), all A*-based methods achieve identical 56.7\% success rates, confirming that heuristic choice affects \emph{which} path is found, not \emph{whether} one exists (Table~\ref{tab:heuristic-performance}). The 3-hop baseline succeeds for only 30\% of start nodes. Start-node sensitivity persists: $\sim$43\% of nodes yield no profitable path regardless of method.

\subsubsection{Comparison Against Baselines}

The 3-hop enumeration baseline finds the same optimal paths as A* when 3-hop paths exist, confirming no path-quality sacrifice. Its complexity $O(|V| \cdot d^3)$ becomes prohibitive for larger graphs (Section~\ref{sec:graph_scaling}). Simple 1-hop and 2-hop baselines and Bellman--Ford all fail to produce profitable paths, validating that our execution-aware graph eliminates theoretical-only opportunities.

\subsubsection{Graph Scaling}
\label{sec:graph_scaling}

We scale from 4 to 12 exchanges (19--41~nodes, 161--864~edges, 10~trials each). Table~\ref{tab:graph_scaling} shows that Dijkstra's expansions remain flat (341--404) while $h_1$ and $h_4$ expand 19--130\% more nodes due to penalty-driven exploration. The 3-hop baseline exhibits high variance (67--662 evaluations), contrasting with monotonic A* scaling.

\subsubsection{Quote Staleness and Path Survival}
\label{sec:quote_staleness}

We re-evaluate discovered paths against fresh market data after delays of 5--300\,s across 270 measurements. \textbf{99.6\%} of paths remain profitable up to 120\,s; only at 300\,s does a single path lose profitability (98\% survival). This demonstrates strong \emph{structural} persistence of stablecoin arbitrage paths, driven by the slowly-changing fee landscape. We note that CCXT-level ticker caching may mask true price-change risk; historical tick-data replay would strengthen this finding (see Table~\ref{tab:staleness} and Figure~\ref{fig:staleness}).

\subsubsection{Temporal Robustness: 8-Hour Overnight Test}
\label{sec:overnight}

We run all five methods from 5~start nodes at three cash levels over 96~snapshots (8~hours, 5-min intervals), yielding \textbf{7{,}200 search instances}. All A*-based methods achieve \textbf{100\% success}; 3-hop enumeration succeeds in only 39.8\% of runs. Table~\ref{tab:overnight_pathtype} shows that $h_2$ produces the highest cross-exchange path ratio (95.7\%), while $h_4$ and $h_1$ more frequently settle on intra-exchange trades.

\subsubsection{Sensitivity Analysis}
\label{sec:sensitivity}

We conduct parameter sweeps on the 12-exchange graph.

\paragraph{$h_1$ penalty weight.}
Sweeping $\lambda_{\text{liq}} \in \{0.1, 0.5, 1.0, 2.0, 5.0\}$ (25~trials each), profit and success rate are invariant (80\%, \$27.77). Expansions vary by only 5.6\%, indicating the penalty primarily reorders the open list.

\paragraph{$h_2$ slippage parameters.}
A joint sweep of $(\lambda_{\text{slip}}, \theta)$ across 100~configurations shows a broad plateau of high performance at moderate values ($\lambda_{\text{slip}} \in [0.3, 1.0]$, $\theta \in [5, 20]$\,bps), confirming $h_2$ is not sensitive to precise tuning (Figure~\ref{fig:sens_h2}).
