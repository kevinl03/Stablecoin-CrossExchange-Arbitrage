We presented an execution-aware A* search framework for cross-exchange stablecoin arbitrage that integrates real-world trading costs—fees, slippage, withdrawal costs, gas fees, transfer latency, and exchange reliability—directly into a graph-based pathfinding formulation. Three domain-specific guidance heuristics ($h_1$, $h_2$, $h_4$) and a multi-start search-control strategy ($h_3$) were evaluated on a graph constructed from 12 major centralized exchanges (41~nodes, 864~edges) across a total of \textbf{7{,}200 overnight search instances}, 270 sensitivity configurations, and 200 graph-scaling trials.

\paragraph{Core contribution: execution-aware graph formulation.}
The primary contribution of this work is the graph construction itself. By encoding real taker fees, withdrawal costs, gas fees, and transfer latency into edge weights using the log-cost formulation $w(e) = -\log(r(e))$, any shortest-path algorithm produces execution-ready arbitrage paths. Dijkstra ($h{=}0$) discovers high-profit paths efficiently on this graph, achieving 100\% success across 1{,}440 overnight runs with mean profit of \$42.87. This establishes that the value lies in the execution-aware modelling, not in any single heuristic.

\paragraph{Heuristic contributions: differentiated roles.}
Our experiments reveal that each heuristic serves a distinct purpose:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{$h_2$ (slippage)}: The most effective heuristic for \emph{search efficiency}. It reduces node expansions by 29--36\% relative to Dijkstra while maintaining near-identical profit and the highest cross-exchange path ratio (95.7\% in overnight runs). However, its reliance on live order-book API calls creates P99 latencies of 6.8\,s—unsuitable for real-time execution.
    \item \textbf{$h_4$ (Weighted A*)}: The most effective heuristic for \emph{real-time deployment}. Pre-computed chain transfer times and static exchange reliability scores provide risk-aware guidance at P99 latency of 33\,ms—comparable to Dijkstra (17\,ms). It expands more nodes and finds lower-profit paths, but its zero-overhead latency profile makes it the only viable heuristic for live trading.
    \item \textbf{$h_1$ (liquidity)}: Useful at small order sizes (11\% expansion reduction at \$1k) but counterproductive at large sizes (99\% more expansions at \$100k). Its penalty is invariant to the $\lambda$ parameter across a $50\times$ range, suggesting the penalty formulation needs refinement.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Empirical findings.}
Profitable paths consistently exhibit a 2--3~hop structure, with net returns of ${\sim}0.09\%$ after all fees. Approximately 20\% of discovered paths are intra-exchange single trades exploiting minor stablecoin depegging, while the remaining 80\% are genuine cross-exchange arbitrage routes. Opportunities persist across all 96~snapshots of the 8-hour overnight campaign. Sensitivity analysis confirms robustness to hyperparameter choices and linear profit scaling with order size (\$0.09 at \$100 to \$86 at \$100k). Graph scaling experiments (4--12 exchanges) show that A*-based methods scale monotonically while the 3-hop enumeration baseline exhibits high variance.

Quote staleness analysis demonstrates structural path persistence (99.6\% survival at 300\,s delay), though we note that CCXT-level ticker caching may mask true price-change risk, and a historical tick-data replay would provide stronger evidence.

\subsection{Future Work}

Several directions emerge from our findings. First, \emph{decoupling heuristic data acquisition from search}: $h_2$'s strong pruning but poor latency suggests an architecture where order-book data is pre-fetched asynchronously, enabling $h_2$-quality guidance at $h_4$-class latency. Second, extending the framework to \emph{decentralized exchanges (DEXs)} and automated market makers (AMMs), where slippage is deterministic via bonding curves and can be precisely modelled within the heuristic. Third, \emph{historical tick-data replay} to rigorously quantify price-change risk for the thin margins observed. Finally, exploring \emph{adaptive heuristic selection}: since $h_2$ excels at search pruning and $h_4$ excels at latency, a system that dynamically selects the heuristic based on available data freshness and execution deadline could achieve the best of both.

\subsection{Limitations}

The current framework has not executed live trades on the blockchain for the presented data. Although we use real market pricing and fees, the profitability of a planned path may change between planning and execution. In practice, stablecoin markets rebalance under execution pressure, and order-book depth can be consumed by other market participants during the execution window.

Approximately 20\% of discovered ``arbitrage'' paths are intra-exchange single trades that do not require cross-exchange transfers and are discoverable by trivial price comparisons. While these are real profit opportunities, they do not exercise the cross-exchange pathfinding that is the focus of this work.

Additionally, our exchange reliability scores are static priors based on historical uptime and community sentiment, which may not capture sudden exchange failures or regulatory actions. Future work should explore real-time reliability estimation from on-chain and off-chain signals.
